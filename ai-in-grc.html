New area

Using AI in GRC work

AI can speed up a lot of GRC work, but the auditor still owns the result. This area will teach you where AI helps, how to check its output, where it should not be trusted, and how to use it responsibly with sensitive data.

The rule throughout: AI drafts, you decide. Every test, check and finding still needs evidence a reviewer can follow.

Planned modules

Planned

Writing audit tests from policy scope

Use AI to turn a policy into a set of audit tests, then check the tests actually cover every requirement in scope.

  • Map each "shall" statement in a Northbay policy to at least one test
  • Spot gaps: requirements with no test, and tests with no requirement
  • Rewrite vague AI tests so they name the evidence, the sample and the pass criteria
2
Planned

Using AI to run checks

Hand AI the repetitive parts of testing: comparing user lists against HR leavers, flagging dormant or privileged accounts, reading through change tickets.

  • Write a clear prompt for a defined check, with the rule it tests against
  • Re-perform a sample of AI results by hand before relying on them
  • Keep a record of the prompt, input and output as audit evidence
3
Planned

Limitations of AI in audit

Know when AI output is wrong, incomplete or not allowed, and how to explain that to a manager who wants it done faster.

  • Made-up control references, clauses and "facts"
  • Confidential or personal data going into tools that aren't approved (GDPR)
  • Missed context: approved exceptions, compensating controls, the story behind the data
  • Professional judgement and sign-off stay with the auditor (ITAF, IIA standards)
4
Planned

Responsible use of AI with audit data

As a security professional you are trusted with sensitive data. You are responsible for making sure AI is used ethically and safely on it, by you and by the teams you audit.

  • Check a tool is approved, and where the data goes, before anything is uploaded
  • Minimise, mask or anonymise personal and confidential data first (GDPR data minimisation)
  • Be open with auditees and your manager about where AI was used in the work
  • Watch for bias and unfair outcomes when AI helps judge people or their access
  • Know the rules that apply: your organisation's AI policy, the EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001
  • Raise it when you see AI being used unsafely, even when it slows the work down
